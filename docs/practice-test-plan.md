# Three music experiments you can repeat

[← Home](../README.md) · [Listening log](../templates/listening-log.md) · [How we check material](editorial-policy.md)

**Status: prepared, not generation-tested.** These are original exercises. No audio output or detector result has been recorded for this plan.

Start with a tool and model available in your account. Record the tool, exact model label, date, relevant settings and displayed credit cost before generating. Keep those settings unchanged when comparing revisions. Suno and MusicMaker accounts, controls and credits are separate.

## 1. A short vocal song

Purpose: check whether a simple verse and chorus remain clear and distinct.

Open Custom mode in [Suno](https://suno.com/create) or the [MusicMaker song generator](https://musicmaker.im/ai-song-generator/). Keep Instrumental off.

**Style**

```text
Warm acoustic folk-pop with fingerpicked guitar, upright bass,
light percussion and one clear lead vocal.
An intimate verse opens into a brighter chorus.
Keep the arrangement sparse so every lyric is easy to hear.
```

**Original lyrics**

```text
[Verse]
Your coat is hanging by the door
The kettle sings like days before
I leave a light beside the stair
And hear your footsteps everywhere

[Chorus]
Come home when the evening turns blue
There is a place at the table for you
No need to know what tomorrow will do
There is a place at the table for you
```

For take B, change only “light percussion” to “no percussion.” Keep the lyrics and other settings unchanged. Compare word clarity, verse-to-chorus contrast and whether removing percussion changes the feel.

## 2. A quiet instrumental for everyday footage

Purpose: make space for a voiceover and check whether the output remains instrumental.

Use Custom mode, turn Instrumental on and leave Lyrics empty.

**Style**

```text
An instrumental acoustic piece for a quiet everyday scene.
Fingerpicked guitar leads, with upright bass and light strings.
Keep the mood warm and hopeful. No singing or spoken voice.
```

This is the exercise used in the homepage tutorial. For take B, remove only “and light strings.” Listen for any voice-like material, busy passages and a usable beginning and ending. If you compare against narration, use the same narration and playback levels for both takes.

## 3. Change one section of your own song

Purpose: check how much a local edit changes the surrounding music.

Use a generated song from experiment 1 or another song you own. In a tool that offers section editing, select a chorus and record its exact time range. The [Suno editing guide](official-suno-guide.md) links the official controls. Do not describe whole-song regeneration as a local edit.

**Edit request**

```text
In the selected chorus, replace the light percussion with handclaps.
Keep the lyrics, lead vocal, tempo and the rest of the arrangement unchanged.
```

Save the original and edited versions. Compare the requested change, the two edit boundaries, and an unselected verse. Record unintended changes as well as improvements. If your tool or plan lacks section editing, record that condition and leave this experiment pending.

## What to publish after a run

Use the [listening log](../templates/listening-log.md). Keep private account and payment records out of the repository.

| Field | Record |
|:--|:--|
| Tool, model and date | Exact labels observed when generating |
| Input and settings | Full prompt, lyrics, switches and any uploaded material |
| Attempt | Starting version or one specific change |
| Output | Shareable track URL and version identifier |
| Listening notes | Timestamped observations about the criteria above |
| Assessment | What improved, what failed and the next change worth trying |

A failed result is useful evidence. Do not replace it with an unrelated reference song or report a requested change as if it was heard.

## A separate detector workflow check

Use an output you have permission to upload to the [MusicMaker detector](https://musicmaker.im/free-suno-ai-music-detector/). Record its known creation history, file version, date, displayed prediction and AI probability, plus any likely-generator label.

That run can document the upload and result-reading process. Measuring accuracy requires a separate, adequately designed set of independently labelled AI and human recordings; a few demonstration files cannot establish it. See [how to read results](detector-and-release.md).
