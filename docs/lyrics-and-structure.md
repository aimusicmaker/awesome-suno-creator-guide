# Lyrics that leave room to sing

[← Home](../README.md) · [First recipe](../prompts/01-last-train-home.md)

A verse gives the listener a scene. A chorus returns to the central thought. A bridge can change the perspective before the final chorus.

## Start with a small scene

Instead of “I am lonely and miss you,” choose an object, an action, and something left unsaid: a second cup cooling on the counter, a chair nobody moves, an unfinished message. Build your own details around those observations.

The repository's lyric sketches are original. They are deliberately short so you can hear the central idea before spending credits on a larger arrangement.

## Turn a sketch into a full song

```text
[Verse 1]
Four short lines: where are we, and what happened?

[Chorus]
Two to four short lines: the thought worth repeating.

[Verse 2]
A new detail or consequence, rather than Verse 1 rewritten.

[Chorus]
Repeat the central words so the hook becomes familiar.

[Bridge]
A change of view, a question, or a decision.

[Final Chorus]
Return to the hook; change one line if the story has moved.

[Outro]
A final phrase or instrumental ending.
```

This is a **writing worksheet**. Replace the explanatory sentences with actual lyrics before pasting into a generator. The section labels can stay as cues, though models may interpret them differently.

## An original second verse for Last Train Home

```text
[Verse 2]
The corner store has changed its name
The man inside still knows my face
I buy the bread you used to choose
And watch the snow melt off my shoes
```

Place this after the first chorus, then repeat the chorus. It adds a new moment instead of explaining the feeling again.

## Make the words easier to sing

Read them out loud over a steady tap. If one line takes twice as long as its neighbors, shorten it before asking the singer to go faster. Keep important names clear. For Chinese, listen to phrase boundaries and pronunciation; do not assume line breaks alone preserve natural speech.

If a direction is sung aloud, remove it from Lyrics and put a short sound description in Style. If words still rush, simplify the lyric rather than piling on bracket tags.
